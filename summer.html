June · July · August

Six places that are at their best when the north tilts toward the sun.

These are chosen for weather you can plan around: dry heat with a sea breeze, alpine air that stays cool, and the tropics in their rainless months. The bracketed line under every photo is the argument for that place this season — the tags tell you whether it feeds your poetry, history or nature appetite.

Campania, Italy

The Amalfi Coast

  • History
  • Sun & sea
  • Walking

Seasonal norms — highs 27–31 °C, sea 24–27 °C, 12–13 hours of sun, one to three rain days a month from June to August.

Amalfi was a maritime republic long before the postcard view: its ships, coin and maritime law tables shaped Mediterranean trade, and the cathedral crypt still holds the relics of St Andrew, brought from Constantinople in the early 1200s. The coast was inscribed as a cultural landscape because those terraces and towers were built by people, not by a resort developer.

Walk the Sentiero degli Dei high above the sea early in the morning, spend the hottest hours in a Ravello garden, and take the ferry between towns so the road never costs you an afternoon.

[
  • The meltemi wind blows off the Aegean every afternoon through the peak heat
  • Essentially no rain all summer, so nothing in the itinerary is weather-dependent
  • One island gives sunset, caldera rim walks and a Bronze Age city
]

Cyclades, Greece

Santorini

  • Nature
  • History
  • Sun

Seasonal norms — highs 28–30 °C, sea 23–25 °C, 12 hours of sun, under two rain days a month in June through August.

The island is a wound in the earth: a volcanic eruption around 1600 BCE emptied the chamber and the sea poured in, leaving the caldera you look down on from Fira. At Akrotiri, that ash preserved a Minoan town with streets, drains and frescoes — a settlement you can walk through at shoulder height.

Assyrtiko vines are coiled into baskets on black lava to keep their fruit off the wind, which is why the wine tastes like the place. Stay on the quiet east side and drive the rim at golden hour.

[
  • Summer days sit at 20–24 °C while most of the world is melting
  • Rockfall lakes only thaw in June — the turquoise is a summer-only sight
  • Long daylight: sun up around 05:40, down near 21:30 in July
]

Alberta, Canada

Banff & Lake Louise

  • Nature
  • Mountains
  • Cool summer

Seasonal norms — daytime highs 19–24 °C, nights 4–8 °C, July the driest month, snow gone from the valley floors by late June.

Canada's first national park was drawn around a hot spring in 1885, and the reason the lakes are that colour is glacier flour — rock ground to silt by moving ice, suspended in meltwater and turning it milky jade. The colour is at its strongest in high summer, when the ice is melting hardest.

Book the Parks shuttle for Moraine Lake before it sells out, then trade the famous shoreline for a two hour climb to a pass nobody photographs. Carry one warm layer for every summer day here.

[
  • 18–21 °C in July, and the sea air means heat waves rarely reach the city
  • Sixteen-plus hours of daylight leave whole evenings for reading and walking
  • A literature-shaped city: one mile of closes holds three novelists' beginnings
]

Scotland, United Kingdom

Edinburgh

  • Poetry
  • History
  • Long daylight

Seasonal norms — highs 17–21 °C, nights 10–12 °C, daylight from about 04:30 to 22:00 at midsummer; bright spells between passing showers.

Robert Louis Stevenson was born in the New Town and set his body-snatching story in these closes; Arthur Conan Doyle learned to read people in the medical school and gave it to Sherlock Holmes. Edinburgh became UNESCO's first City of Literature because the habit never stopped — the Old Town is still full of rooms that writers rented by the week.

Climb Arthur's Seat at dusk instead of on the crowded midday path, and take the castle rock from the shore side at Portobello, where the whole city explains itself at once.

[
  • Atlantic breezes keep the city a few degrees cooler than inland Iberia
  • Almost no rain in July and August, and the light lasts past 21:00
  • Cheapest good summer capital in western Europe, and walkable in the shade
]

Portugal

Lisbon

  • Poetry
  • History
  • Sun

Seasonal norms — highs 25–31 °C, ocean 18–20 °C, around zero rain days in July and August, nights cooling to 17 °C on a breeze.

Lisbon's age of discovery is carved into Belém, where Manueline stonework strings rope and coral around the windows of a monastery paid for by the spice trade. The 1755 earthquake and the grid that replaced it are why the Baixa feels so wide and so deliberately new.

Fernando Pessoa wrote the city from its Baixa cafés, inventing half a dozen authors to do it, and Alfama still sounds like his saudades on a Thursday night. Ride tram 28 early, then walk it back down.

[
  • June to September is Bali's dry season, so this is the island without the downpours
  • Inland and elevated: cooler nights than the beach, around 21 °C
  • Water, temples and dance on one schedule — you can be tired by 21:00 in a good way

Gianyar, Bali, Indonesia

Ubud

  • Nature
  • Craft & dance
  • Dry season

Seasonal norms — days 27–30 °C, nights 20–22 °C, humidity down and rain near zero from June to September; sunrise about 06:15.

The terraces are engineering, not scenery: subak irrigation channels split one river across a hillside by contract between temples, a system old enough to be on the World Heritage list. Green up to the horizon in July, and gold where the harvest has started by September.

Legong dancers train in the same villages that carve the masks and paint the panels, so the art here is a working trade rather than a shop. Walk the Campuhan ridge at dawn, before the heat decides.
