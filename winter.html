December · January · February

Winter travel pays in empty courtyards, snow and very short days.

These six earn the cold: two give you reliable snow, one gives you the aurora, one is a mild escape where December reads like April, and two are literary cities that are quieter and cheaper than they ever are in summer. The bracketed line under each photo tells you why this place works in winter specifically.

Bohemia, Czechia

Prague

  • Poetry
  • History
  • Snow

Seasonal norms — December to February highs 0 to 4 °C, nights -2 to -6 °C, snow on the ground for weeks at a time, daylight ending near 16:00 in December.

Rainer Maria Rilke was born here and wrote this city's Jewish quarter into his earliest poems; Kafka was born facing the Old Town square and set his trials in its corridors. It is a place you visit for a sentence you already know.

The bridge began in 1357 and the astronomical clock has been marking the hours since 1410, which is why the historic centre is protected rather than merely pretty. Cross it at first light in snow and you will have it to yourself.

[
  • The aurora only shows against a dark sky, and November to January supplies most of the day
  • A capital city base: you can be warm by 22:00 even after a midnight sky watch
  • Geothermal heat makes a 0 °C day feel like a mild one, outdoors and indoors
]

Capital Region, Iceland

Reykjavík

  • Nature
  • Poetry
  • Dark sky

Seasonal norms — highs -1 to 2 °C, nights -5 °C, windier than the temperature suggests; roughly 4 hours of daylight at the end of December, 7 by late January, and full darkness from late evening for aurora watching.

Iceland's winter is the season the literature was written in: the sagas and the Eddas were composed through long dark winters by farms that had nothing else to do, and Halldór Laxness carried that inheritance to a Nobel Prize. The northernmost capital of a sovereign state still reads like a country of storytellers.

Chase the sky rather than a schedule. Cloud breaks are what matter, so keep the evening free, drive out past the city lights, and let the geothermal pool be the plan B.

[
  • Snow-covered from November to April and the only car-free village in the list
  • The rack railway to Gornergrat gives you thirteen peaks over 4,000 m in one look
  • Days are cold but dry — the alpine sun at 2,500 m is the warmest thing on the trip

Valais, Switzerland

Zermatt & the Matterhorn

  • Nature
  • Snow
  • Mountains

Seasonal norms — village highs -2 to 3 °C, nights to -10 °C, strong sun at altitude and the driest cold season of the Swiss Alps.

The Matterhorn was climbed in 1865 and the seven who came down alive changed mountaineering's ambition; the mountain's four-thousand-metre tooth is the reason this valley is known at all. Combustion engines are barred from the village streets, so the traffic is electric carts and boots.

Take the Gornergrat line, open in 1890 and still the highest open-air cog railway in Europe, for the view that makes the skiing optional. Book a room you can see the peak from and let the clouds decide your morning.

[
  • December to February is summer here — a northern winter holiday that runs at 22 °C
  • If you want the cold instead, June to August gives you snow and empty ski fields
  • Lake, mountains and daylight all in one valley: nothing in the week depends on a drive
]

Otago, New Zealand

Queenstown

  • Nature
  • Summer in winter
  • Snow in June

Seasonal norms — December to February highs 20–26 °C with long dry evenings; June to August highs 5–8 °C in the town and reliable snow on the surrounding ranges.

The town sits on a lightning-bolt lake boxed in by the Southern Alps, and it invented commercial bungy jumping from an old gold-rush bridge in 1988 — an alpine adventure habit that has never slowed down since. The Remarkables and Coronet Peak are the snow side of the same year.

Take the gondola the hour before sunset, when the light moves down the valley floor and the lake turns grey-blue. Then eat late; summer evenings here run past 21:00.

  • December and February feel like spring: 18–21 °C, almost no rain, cold only at night
  • The medina's roof terraces become usable again, which is the whole pleasure of the city
  • Fly in for four days and it still feels long — nothing here needs a car or a schedule

Morocco

Marrakech

  • History
  • Mild winter
  • Sun

Seasonal norms — December to February highs 18–21 °C, nights 5–7 °C, and only a few rain days in a month; hot, bright, and dry at noon.

The walled medina is World Heritage, the Koutoubia minaret is twelfth-century, and the Saadian tombs stayed sealed behind a wall until they were found again in the early 1900s. Jemaa el-Fnaa is recognised by UNESCO for something less solid and more alive: the circle of storytellers who hold the square every night in verse, genealogy and diagnosis.

Winter is when the souk is bearable at 14:00. Buy the cool end of the day for the square, and the first hour of morning for the gardens before the tour groups find the light.

  • Golden Pavilion in snow is the single most famous picture in Japan, and winter is when it happens
  • Temple grounds that are unbearable in August are empty and cold enough to sit in
  • Onsen towns an hour away, and the food is best precisely when it is coldest

Kansai, Japan

Kyoto

  • Poetry
  • History
  • Snow

Seasonal norms — December to February highs 8–10 °C, nights 0–2 °C; dry, bright cold, with a handful of real snowfalls that the temples are built to catch.

Kyoto was the imperial capital from 794, which means its poetry anthologies, garden stones and seasonal vocabulary were all assembled here. Bashō's frog-pond verse is remembered at Rakuō, the little hermitage garden in the north, and the waka poets wrote more honestly about snow than about cherry blossom.

The pavilion was built as a retired shogun's gold-leaf villa in 1397 and rebuilt in 1955 after a fire; in snow, the gold goes quiet and the pond goes mirror-flat. Go early — the first hour opens at 09:00.
